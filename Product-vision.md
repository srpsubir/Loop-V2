# Loop — Product Vision

> Read this document at the start of every session, after every /compact, before making any product or design decision.

---

## The Brief (internal only — never appears in product or marketing)

There is a loneliness epidemic. It is one of the most significant public health crises of our time. But the people living through it don't experience it as an epidemic. They experience it as:

- "I keep meaning to call her."
- "We used to talk every week."
- "I don't even know what he's up to anymore."
- "Time just passed and now it feels weird to reach out."

Nobody registers the label. Everybody recognizes the feeling.

**The epidemic is the brief. It informs every decision. It never appears in the product.**

What appears instead: the felt micro-moment. The specific, embodied drift that everyone has lived but never named. When the product speaks that language — exactly, without dressing it up — the reader thinks: *that's me.* That recognition is the entire acquisition strategy.

Loop exists because staying close to the people who matter most requires effort nobody has. Not because people stop caring. Because life makes closeness passive, and nobody keeps count.

---

## The Vision (locked — do not reopen)

Loop is an intelligent relationship companion that holds two things simultaneously:

1. **The people you want to stay close to** — relationship intelligence, nudges, signal. Who needs you right now?
2. **The identities you lived through while you were with them** — chapters, memory, warmth. Who were you back then?

Neither dimension subordinates the other. Same underlying data. Two lenses.

The People layer answers: *"Who do I want to maintain a relationship with, right now?"*
The Chapter layer answers: *"Who was I during that period of my life, and who was there with me?"*

**What makes Loop distinct:** Every other product picks one dimension. LinkedIn is people-first. Instagram is chapter-first. Google Photos is chapter-first. Loop holds both, because real relationships are inseparable from the identities that existed when you were in them.

## Jobs To Be Done

**JTBD 2 — Losing touch (primary acquisition hook)**
> "I'm beginning to lose touch with my closest friends, but I don't have time to schedule stuff with them — I need a gentle nudge, without it feeling like a chore."

This is what drives downloads. This is the pain people feel and can't name. This is the brief in action.

**JTBD 1 — Nostalgia (retention soul)**
> "I want to relive the best chapters of my life and feel the warmth of who I was with."

This is what makes people stay. This is why Loop is not just a nudge app. The chapter layer is the depth that earns long-term loyalty.

**The acquisition order:** JTBD 2 opens the door. JTBD 1 is what they find inside.

## 1. Positioning

**The gap Loop fills:** Most loneliness solutions are about acquisition — meet new people, find community, make friends. Loop is the answer to the other problem: **don't lose the people you already love.**

That gap is unowned. Nobody says it yet.

**Positioning anchor:**
> "The people who matter most are already in your life. Loop makes sure they stay there."

**What Loop is not:**
- Not a reminder app (implies you forgot, creates guilt)
- Not a CRM (implies people are contacts to manage)
- Not a journaling app (implies you do the work)
- Not a social feed (implies broadcasting)

Loop does the watching. You just live.

**Tagline direction:** "The people who matter most, quietly kept close."

**The word "loneliness" never appears in any user-facing surface.** Not in copy, not in notifications, not in onboarding. The product speaks the language of the felt moment instead.

---

## 2. Onboarding Sequence

Five beats. People-first, then chapters. Emotion before product.

**Beat 1 — The felt moment (no input, no product)**
A full-screen statement. One thing. No button to skip past too quickly.

> "There are people in your life you'd do anything for. When did you last actually talk to all of them?"

No statistic. No label. Just the true thing, landing. The user either feels it or they don't — and the ones who feel it are the ones Loop is for.

**Beat 2 — Normalize the drift**
> "It's not that you forgot. Life just moves. Staying close takes effort nobody has."

One sentence. Then: "Loop takes care of that for you."

**Beat 3 — Name your people**
"Who do you want to make sure you never lose touch with?"

Contact picker. 3 to 5 people. No declaration framing — the user showing up is already the act. They don't need to perform a promise on top of it. Treat this as a simple, warm selection: pick faces from your contacts, see them appear. The friction is low; the stakes feel real without being named.

No "I commit to staying close to..." language. No ritual. Just: who?

**Beat 4 — Grant access**
"We need to see where the silence is." Contacts + WhatsApp. One sentence on why. No feature list.

**Beat 5 — First truth**
Show them something real immediately. Not a walkthrough. Not a tour. A live signal: here's one person you designated, here's how long it's been. The product already knows something true about their life.

**Beat 6 — The chapter layer (last, not first)**
"As you go, we'll save the chapters of your life — the crews, the eras, the people who were there." This is the depth. It earns its place after the user has already committed to the people layer.

**What onboarding never does:**
- Opens with a feature tour
- Uses a statistic about loneliness or disconnection
- Makes the user feel guilty
- Starts with the chapter/nostalgia layer

## 3. Home Screen Hierarchy

Top to bottom, in this order:

**Greeting + date.** One line. Warm. Anchors the user in the present. Not functional — human.

**Your people (Stay Close strip).** Faces. First names. Days since last contact. The close relationships surface immediately on open — no navigation, no category tap. Horizontal scroll, not a list. Personal, like a film strip, not a task queue.

**One signal card.** A single active nudge. Not a notification center. One thing worth acting on right now. "Message on WhatsApp" or "Dismiss." The nudge copy lives in the felt register:

- Not: "You haven't spoken to Priya in 3 weeks." (task, clinical)
- Yes: "Priya's been quiet in your life lately." (presence, human)

The difference: a friend noticing something, not a system alerting.

**The chapter layer.** Below the fold or secondary scroll. Beautiful, visual, nostalgic. Not checked daily — but when you do, it's like opening an album.

**Principle:** The app answers two questions. "Who needs me right now?" is on top. "Who was I back then?" is deeper.

## 4. Brand Identity

**Emotional register:** The warmth of a letter from someone who knows you well. Unhurried. Personal. Present without being intrusive.

**The brand takes a position** — not loudly, not as a movement, but quietly. It knows why it exists. It has thought hard about what it means for time to pass and for people to drift. That belief shows in the copy and the visual treatment, not in slogans.

**Color:** Deep terracotta as primary accent. Warm off-white ground — close to natural paper, not clinical white, not harsh black. Shadows feel like late afternoon, not fluorescent. (D-II palette direction: Ground #F4E7E2, Surface #EDD9D2, Card #FFFFFF, Ink #1A100C, Terra #B8624A.)

**Typography:** Serif for emotional moments — greetings, person names at key moments, chapter titles. Clean sans for UI chrome and functional data. The serif carries feeling; the sans carries information. Never reverse those roles.

**Motion:** Slow, weighted. Nothing bouncy, nothing gamified. Transitions like turning a page. The product breathes; it doesn't perform.

**Voice:** Second person, present tense, direct. The product notices things the way a close friend would — not as an alarm, not as a task. Never clinical. Never guilty.

**What the brand never looks like:**
- A productivity dashboard
- A CRM with scores and metrics
- A social feed with likes and comments
- A gamified habit tracker

**The manifesto paragraph** (lives on landing page and about screen):
> "Time passes. People get busy. Silence accumulates. Not because anyone stopped caring — because staying close takes effort nobody has, and nobody keeps count. Loop keeps count. Quietly."

The word "loneliness" does not appear in this paragraph or anywhere near it.

---

## 5. Landing Page

**Structure: recognition before product.**

**Above the fold — the felt moment.**
No statistic. No movement language. A moment everyone recognizes:

> "The people you love most — when did you last talk to all of them?"

Beneath it, one line: "Loop keeps them close, without the mental load."

CTA: "Join the waitlist" or "Download for Mac"

The question does the work. The reader either feels it or they don't. The ones who feel it download the app.

**Second section — product truth.**
Show what it actually does. Not a feature carousel. A real moment: a face, a name, "hasn't been in touch in 3 weeks," a "Say hi on WhatsApp" button. Make it feel true, not polished.

**Third section — the manifesto.**
The belief paragraph from Brand Identity above. One column. Editorial treatment. Dark background, warm type. This is where Loop takes its position — quietly, without naming the epidemic.

**Fourth section — the chapter layer.**
Introduce the second dimension. "And years from now, you'll have a record of who you were and who was with you." Warmth, nostalgia. The reason people stay.

**Fifth section — social proof.**
One or two specific quotes from real users. Not "great app." Something like: "I called my best friend after two years because of this." Specific. Emotional. True.

**Footer:** Tagline. Permanent.

**What the landing page never does:**
- Opens with a loneliness statistic
- Uses the word "epidemic," "crisis," or "isolated"
- Leads with features before the emotional recognition
- Looks like a productivity or CRM product

## What Does Not Change

- The dual-layer architecture: People layer + Chapter layer, both first-class
- The chapter detection algorithm and orbit/card visual metaphor
- WhatsApp as the action layer (message on WhatsApp)
- The warmth tier system (close / warm)
- "Not a reminder. A memory." — this line survives as the chapter layer's payoff, not the primary hook
